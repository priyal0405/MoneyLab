      💸 What is an expense ratio?

      It's the yearly fee a mutual fund charges to manage your money. It's shown as a percentage of what you've invested.

      🧾

      How it works

      With a 1% expense ratio, a fund takes about ₹100 a year from every ₹10,000 you've invested. It pays the fund manager, research, admin and, in Regular plans, the agent who sold you the fund.

      🫥

      You never see a bill

      The fee is taken out a little each day, before the fund's price (NAV) is published. You won't get a separate charge. Your returns are just slightly lower. The returns on this page already have the fee taken out.

      🐢

      Small % = big money over time

      Put in ₹1 lakh for 20 years. If the market earns 12% a year:
      at 0.2% fee → about ₹9.3 lakh
      at 1.5% fee → about ₹7.4 lakh
      That 1.3% difference costs you about ₹1.9 lakh.

      Rough guide: index funds often charge 0.1–0.5%, and actively managed funds 0.5–2%. Lower isn't automatically better, because a pricier fund can still earn more. But you should know what you're paying.

      Direct vs Regular: it's the same fund. A Regular plan costs more because it includes a commission for the agent or app that sold it. Over many years, a Direct plan usually leaves you with more money.

      Where to find it: the fund's monthly factsheet, the fund company's website, or apps like Groww and Kuvera. Look for "TER" (Total Expense Ratio). Type it in the boxes above to see it in the comparison.

      How we worked this out

      • Data: daily NAVs from mfapi.in, a free service based on AMFI data.
      • Returns: 3- and 5-year returns are yearly averages (CAGR), calculated from NAVs. They're blank if the fund is too young.
      • Volatility: how much the daily NAV moved over the last 3 years, scaled to a year.
      • Worst fall: the largest drop from a peak to a low in the last 5 years.
